# Content Audit

## Goal

Audit the current `software-online-review.com` content direction so the new review platform can reuse what is valuable and avoid importing what creates noise.

## High-Level Read

The current live site appears to be a hybrid of:

- software and tooling content
- Project SOR brand narrative
- broad technical and business topics
- personal or loosely related posts
- portfolio-style and asset-sale signaling

This means the content base has value, but it is not yet aligned to a clean review-product structure.

## Core Audit Principle

Do not migrate content one-to-one.

Instead, classify existing content by whether it helps:

- software discovery
- software buyer intent
- platform trust
- brand narrative
- future SEO expansion

## Recommended Content Buckets

### Keep

Content that already supports the product direction.

Examples:

- software-related posts
- tool comparisons
- platform-specific insight posts
- category-oriented discovery content
- product or technology explainers with clear buyer relevance

Use for:

- SEO support
- internal linking into software pages
- category landing pages

### Rewrite

Content with useful intent but weak current structure.

Examples:

- broad posts that mention software but are not structured
- posts that can become software category pages
- posts that can be turned into product profile descriptions
- content that is valuable but too blog-shaped for the new app

Use for:

- software profile copy
- category intros
- app landing pages

### Archive

Content that has historical value but does not help the review product directly.

Examples:

- old experimental posts
- scattered reflections that do not support product discovery
- posts kept only for continuity or archive value

Use for:

- preserving history without cluttering the new app

### Ignore

Content that should not influence the new product direction.

Examples:

- unrelated lifestyle or personal posts
- content with no connection to software discovery
- low-value or confusing one-off items

## What Looks Strongest Today

From the current workspace and domain patterns, the strongest content directions seem to be:

- software tooling and discovery
- business software and technical stack categories
- Project SOR framing around software ecosystems
- comparative or future-oriented product narratives

These are the best raw materials for the new platform.

## What Looks Weakest Today

The weakest fit for the new platform appears to be:

- overly broad multi-topic publishing
- content that feels unrelated to software reviews
- asset-sale messaging mixed into user-facing product pages
- loosely connected personal content mixed with discovery flows

## Recommended Audit Output

For each major content area, decide one of these actions:

- keep as blog or SEO content
- rewrite into category page support
- rewrite into software profile support
- archive
- ignore

## Migration Rules

### Rule 1

Do not move blog posts into the app unless they become structured product-supporting content.

### Rule 2

Do not create software profiles just because a post exists.

### Rule 3

Do not keep public messaging that confuses the platform with a personal or resale asset.

### Rule 4

Preserve useful content as a traffic source, not as a substitute for product structure.

## Suggested First Audit Pass

Review existing content in this order:

1. software-specific posts
2. category and ecosystem posts
3. Project SOR narrative pages
4. generic technical posts
5. unrelated personal or mixed-theme posts

## Output Format Recommendation

For the real audit sheet, use columns like:

- URL or slug
- title
- main topic
- software relevance
- action: keep, rewrite, archive, ignore
- target destination
- notes

## Success Criteria

The audit is successful when:

- the live site keeps useful SEO value
- the new app only gets structured and relevant content
- user-facing messaging becomes more coherent
- the future platform feels focused instead of sprawling
